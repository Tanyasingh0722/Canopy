The one thing to fix in Figma
1
Move ellipse centre up, not down
Select your shadow ellipse. Move it so its vertical centre is about 10–14px above the bottom edge of your object — not below it, not at the same level. The bottom half of the ellipse peeks out below the object; the top half is hidden behind it. This is what creates the "tucked under" look.
2
Check layer order — shadow must be below object
In the Layers panel: shadow ellipse must sit below your illustration layer. If it's above, it'll show on top of the object. Right-click → Send to Back, or drag it below in the Layers panel.
3
Make ellipse wider than the object, not the same width
Shadow width ≈ 1.3–1.5× the object width. In the reference, shadows always spread a little past the object edges on both sides — this is what makes them read as cast light, not a floor sticker.
4
Keep horizontal offset small — 5–8px max
The reference shadows barely move right. Use X offset: +6px from the object centre. Any more and it detaches visually. The subtle offset is enough to imply light direction without the shadow reading as a separate object.
Quick check: if you can see a gap of empty background between your object and the shadow, the ellipse is too low. Move it up until the shadow appears to emerge from underneath the object, not sit beneath it.
